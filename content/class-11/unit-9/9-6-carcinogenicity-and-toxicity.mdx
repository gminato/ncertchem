## Benzene and the polynuclear hydrocarbons

Benzene is toxic. It is the simplest member of the aromatic family, and the toxicity starts with a single ring. The hydrocarbons that cause cancer are the polynuclear ones: hydrocarbons with more than two benzene rings fused together, so that neighbouring rings share an edge.

These hydrocarbons form on incomplete combustion of organic materials such as tobacco, coal and petroleum. Once they enter the body they undergo biochemical reactions, and the end products finally damage DNA and cause cancer. ## The rule to remember

NCERT's statement is precise. Benzene is toxic, and polynuclear hydrocarbons containing more than two benzene rings fused together are toxic and carcinogenic. The rule for the polynuclear family is the number of fused rings: two rings fused together do not meet the statement. That is why the two drawings in the Scrolly have four and five rings.

NCERT names the family and says what the molecules do in the body. It does not explain the damage to DNA in detail, and this Section does not either. Your task is to recognise the family from its rings.
