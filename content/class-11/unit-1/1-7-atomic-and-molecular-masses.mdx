<Hook>
The periodic table gives chlorine a mass of about 35.5 u. But no single chlorine atom weighs exactly that. How can an atom have a fractional mass, and what is the table really reporting?
</Hook>

Atoms are far too light to weigh one at a time, so chemists compare them. Hydrogen, the lightest atom, was once given a relative mass of 1 and the others were measured against it. Today the standard is carbon-12, and every atomic mass is given relative to it.

**Atomic mass.** An atom of carbon-12, written $^{12}\text{C}$, is assigned a mass of exactly 12 u. The **unified mass unit**, u, is one-twelfth of its mass. In grams, $1\ \text{u} = 1.66056 \times 10^{-24}\ \text{g}$. This unit replaced the older amu. On this scale a hydrogen atom weighs about 1.008 u.

**Average atomic mass.** Most elements occur naturally as a mixture of isotopes: atoms with the same number of protons but different masses. The atomic mass in the periodic table is the average over this mixture, weighted by how abundant each isotope is. Chlorine is the clearest example.

<Scrolly animation="isotope-seesaw">
<Step>
Chlorine has two stable isotopes: $^{35}\text{Cl}$ with a mass of 34.9689 u, and $^{37}\text{Cl}$ with a mass of 36.9659 u. Of every 100 chlorine atoms, about 76 are $^{35}$Cl and the rest are $^{37}$Cl. The exact abundances are 75.77 % and 24.23 %.
</Step>
<Step>
Think of the two isotopes as weights on a seesaw, placed at their masses along a number line. Start with the pivot at the midpoint, and the plank tips towards the more abundant $^{35}$Cl. Slide the pivot until the plank is level. That balance point is the average atomic mass, 35.45 u.
</Step>
<Step>
The balance point is an abundance-weighted sum. Each isotope contributes its abundance times its mass: $0.7577 \times 34.9689\ \text{u} = 26.496\ \text{u}$ from $^{35}$Cl, and $0.2423 \times 36.9659\ \text{u} = 8.957\ \text{u}$ from $^{37}$Cl. Added together, they give 35.453 u.
</Step>
<Step>
The average atomic mass of chlorine is therefore 35.45 u. It is not the mass of any single atom, which is why it is not a whole number. NCERT's worked examples use 35.5 u for chlorine, the value that gives sodium chloride a formula mass of $23.0 + 35.5 = 58.5\ \text{u}$.
</Step>
</Scrolly>

**Molecular mass.** The mass of a molecule is the sum of the atomic masses of all its atoms. Methane, $\ce{CH4}$, has one carbon and four hydrogen atoms, so its molecular mass is $12.011\ \text{u} + 4 \times 1.008\ \text{u} = 16.043\ \text{u}$. For water, $\ce{H2O}$, the sum is $2 \times 1.008\ \text{u} + 16.00\ \text{u} = 18.016\ \text{u}$, which NCERT quotes as 18.02 u.

<Scrolly animation="molecular-mass">
<Step>
Methane, $\ce{CH4}$, is built from one carbon atom and four hydrogen atoms. Its molecular mass is the sum of the atomic masses of all five atoms. Start a ledger, and add the atoms to it one at a time.
</Step>
<Step>
The carbon atom contributes 12.011 u, so the running total is 12.011 u. The atomic masses here are the averages from the periodic table, which is why carbon carries three decimal places. Next come the hydrogen atoms.
</Step>
<Step>
Four hydrogen atoms at 1.008 u each add 4.032 u. The running total is now 16.043 u, the molecular mass of methane. Adding the atoms one by one gives the same answer as taking the formula $\ce{CH4}$ as a whole.
</Step>
<Step>
Water, $\ce{H2O}$, works the same way: one oxygen atom at 16.00 u and two hydrogen atoms at 1.008 u each. The two hydrogen atoms add only about an eighth of the oxygen's mass, but they are part of the molecule, so the total is 18.016 u.
</Step>
<Step>
Sodium chloride is different. $\ce{Na+}$ and $\ce{Cl-}$ ions pack into a three-dimensional lattice, and each ion is surrounded by six of the opposite kind. There are no discrete $\ce{NaCl}$ molecules, so we quote the **formula mass** of one formula unit: 23.0 u + 35.5 u = 58.5 u.
</Step>
</Scrolly>

<KeyIdeas>
- **Atomic mass unit:** 1 u is one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom, and 1 u = $1.66056 \times 10^{-24}\ \text{g}$.
- **Average atomic mass:** the value in the periodic table is the average over an element's isotopes, weighted by abundance. Chlorine's is 35.45 u.
- **Molecular mass:** the sum of the atomic masses of every atom in a molecule, such as $\ce{CH4}$ at 16.043 u.
- **Formula mass:** for an ionic solid such as $\ce{NaCl}$, with no discrete molecules, the same sum over one formula unit: 58.5 u.
</KeyIdeas>
