<Hook>
Pour a glass of water from a Himalayan spring and another from a Bengaluru tap. They may taste different, but every molecule in both has exactly two hydrogen atoms for each oxygen atom. Why should nature be so strict?
</Hook>

The idea that matter is built from tiny particles is very old. It was discussed in ancient Greece, but for a long time it was only a philosophy. The laws of combination in Section 1.5 changed that, because they could only be explained by a model of matter made of atoms.

In 1808, John Dalton published *A New System of Chemical Philosophy*. In it he set out four ideas. Each one is pictured in a Step below.

<Scrolly animation="dalton-postulates">
<Step>
**Postulate 1: matter consists of indivisible atoms.** A sample is an enormous number of separate particles, here a grid of identical carbon atoms. Dalton treated each atom as the smallest unit of an element, one that cannot be cut into smaller pieces. We now know that atoms have an inner structure, but for chemistry the picture still works.
</Step>
<Step>
**Postulate 2: atoms of one element are identical, and different elements differ.** Every atom of hydrogen is like every other, and so is every atom of oxygen, but hydrogen and oxygen atoms are not alike. This figure uses colour and size as a visual code. Dalton's wording included identical mass, which later work refined: atoms of one element can differ in mass, as isotopes do.
</Step>
<Step>
**Postulate 3: compounds form in fixed, whole-number ratios.** Water always has two H atoms for each O atom, and carbon dioxide has one C for every two O. Each compound has its own fixed ratio, and the ratio is always a whole number. This is why a compound has a definite composition, as the law of definite proportions requires.
</Step>
<Step>
**Postulate 4: reactions rearrange atoms.** In $\ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O}$ the atoms are regrouped, not made or destroyed. Count them before and after: four H and two O on each side. This is the atomic reason for the law of conservation of mass that you met in Section 1.5.
</Step>
</Scrolly>

Dalton's ideas explained the laws of combination of mass. They did not, by themselves, explain the laws of gaseous volumes. For those, the molecules had to be diatomic, which is the step Avogadro took. Dalton also could not say why atoms combine in the way they do; that answer came later, with the study of chemical bonds.

<KeyIdeas>
- **Postulate 1:** matter is made of atoms, which Dalton took to be indivisible, the smallest unit of an element.
- **Postulate 2:** atoms of one element are alike, and differ from atoms of other elements. Isotopes show that the mass part of this idea needed refining.
- **Postulate 3:** compounds form when atoms combine in fixed whole-number ratios, which gives each compound a definite composition.
- **Postulate 4:** a chemical reaction rearranges atoms; they are neither created nor destroyed.
</KeyIdeas>
