## 6.4 Homogeneous equilibria

A reaction is **homogeneous** when every reactant and every product is in one phase. The gas-phase synthesis of ammonia is the standard case:

$$
\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}
$$

Every term of this equilibrium is a gas, so every term enters the equilibrium constant. The same is true in solution. In a clear aqueous mixture of iron(III) and thiocyanate ions, $\ce{Fe^3+(aq) + SCN^-(aq) <=> FeSCN^2+(aq)}$ has all of its species dissolved in one phase, and each of them appears in $K$.

## 6.4.1 Equilibrium constant in gaseous systems

So far $K$ has been written in molar concentrations, $K_c$. For gases it is often more convenient to use partial pressures. The bridge between the two is the ideal gas equation, $pV = nRT$, which can be rearranged with the concentration $[\text{gas}] = n/V$:

$$
p = \frac{n}{V}\,RT = [\text{gas}]\,RT
$$

Here $p$ is in bar, $[\text{gas}]$ is in mol L⁻¹, and $R = 0.0831\ \text{bar L mol}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}$. At a fixed temperature the partial pressure of a gas is proportional to its concentration. The Scrolly below takes one gas, then three equilibria with different numbers of gas moles.

<Scrolly animation="kp-kc">
<Step>
Take a gas at a concentration of $0.100\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$ in a box held at $400\ \text{K}$. Its molecules strike the walls and give a pressure. Multiply the concentration by $RT$, with $RT = 33.24\ \text{L bar mol}^{-1}$, and the pressure comes out directly: $p = 0.100 \times 33.24 = 3.32\ \text{bar}$.
</Step>
<Step>
Start with $\ce{H2(g) + I2(g) <=> 2HI(g)}$. At equilibrium the concentrations are $[\ce{H2}] = [\ce{I2}] = 0.100\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$ and $[\ce{HI}] = 0.800\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$, so $K_c = 64.0$. Two gas moles go in and two come out, so $\Delta n = 0$. The factor $(RT)^0$ is 1, and $K_p = K_c = 64.0$ with no unit.
</Step>
<Step>
Now $\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}$. Four gas moles go in and two come out, so $\Delta n = 2 - 4 = -2$. With $[\ce{N2}] = 0.200$, $[\ce{H2}] = 0.300$ and $[\ce{NH3}] = 0.100\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$, the constant is $K_c = 1.85\ \text{L}^2\,\text{mol}^{-2}$. Then $K_p = 1.85 \times (33.24)^{-2} = 1.68 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{bar}^{-2}$.
</Step>
<Step>
Next, $\ce{2NOCl(g) <=> 2NO(g) + Cl2(g)}$. Two gas moles go in and three come out, so $\Delta n = 3 - 2 = +1$. The concentrations $[\ce{NOCl}] = 0.400$, $[\ce{NO}] = 0.200$ and $[\ce{Cl2}] = 0.100\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$ give $K_c = 0.0250\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$. Here $K_p = 0.0250 \times 33.24 = 0.831\ \text{bar}$.
</Step>
<Step>
Put the three together. A positive $\Delta n$ multiplies $K_c$ by $RT$, and a negative one divides by it. The unit of $K_p$ is bar raised to $\Delta n$: bar for the chlorine case, bar⁻² for ammonia, and no unit when $\Delta n = 0$. Only gases are counted, so a solid or a liquid in the same equation would not change $\Delta n$.
</Step>

### Why the factor is (RT)^Δn

The relation between the two constants comes from writing every partial pressure as $p = [\text{gas}]RT$. For a general reaction $a\ce{A} + b\ce{B} <=> c\ce{C} + d\ce{D}$, all four species are gases, and

$$
K_p = \frac{p_\text{C}^{\,c}\,p_\text{D}^{\,d}}{p_\text{A}^{\,a}\,p_\text{B}^{\,b}} = \frac{[\text{C}]^c[\text{D}]^d}{[\text{A}]^a[\text{B}]^b}\,(RT)^{(c+d)-(a+b)}
$$

The bracket on the right is $K_c$, so the two constants are linked by

$$
K_p = K_c\,(RT)^{\Delta n}
$$

where $\Delta n = (\text{moles of gaseous products}) - (\text{moles of gaseous reactants})$ in the balanced equation. The factor $RT$ appears with the power $\Delta n$, so it cancels exactly when the gas moles are equal on the two sides.

### Units follow Δn

Kp is written with pressures in bar, because the standard state for a pure gas is 1 bar. A pressure of 4 bar in the standard state is $4\ \text{bar}/1\ \text{bar} = 4$, which is a number with no unit. So the unit of $K_p$ is bar raised to $\Delta n$, and $K_c$ has units of mol L⁻¹ raised to $\Delta n$. For $\Delta n = 0$ both constants are unit-less, and for $\Delta n = +1$ or $-2$ they carry bar or mol L⁻¹ with the matching power.

<KeyIdeas>
- In a homogeneous equilibrium every species is in one phase, and every species enters the constant.
- For gases, $p = [\text{gas}]\,RT$ with $R = 0.0831\ \text{bar L mol}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}$, so $K_p = K_c\,(RT)^{\Delta n}$.
- $\Delta n$ counts gaseous moles only: products minus reactants. Zero means $K_p = K_c$.
- Kc is in mol L⁻¹ raised to $\Delta n$, and Kp is in bar raised to $\Delta n$. A pure gas in its standard state is 1 bar.
- Both constants change only with temperature, never with the amount of gas or the pressure.
</KeyIdeas>
