## One idea behind every use

Until now this unit asked how a coordination entity is built, named and bonded. This section asks what it is for. The answer is that surrounding a metal ion with ligands changes its behaviour. A complex can dissolve where the simple ion would not, show a colour the simple ion lacks, bind a metal tightly enough to hide it, or let go of it again when conditions change. Every application below is one of these four effects, applied somewhere useful. NCERT groups the uses under analysis, metallurgy, biology, industry and medicine, and so does this section.

## Analysis: colour and stability as a signal

Many ligands, especially chelating ligands, give a metal ion a distinctive colour on complex formation. That colour reaction is the basis of both detecting a metal (qualitative analysis) and measuring how much is present (quantitative analysis), by classical methods and by instruments. NCERT names EDTA, dimethylglyoxime (DMG), $\alpha$-nitroso-$\beta$-naphthol and cupron among the reagents.

Hardness of water is a clean example. It is estimated by a simple titration with the disodium salt of EDTA, written $\ce{Na2EDTA}$. Calcium and magnesium ions, $\ce{Ca^2+}$ and $\ce{Mg^2+}$, each form a stable complex with EDTA. The two complexes have different stability constants, which is what lets the two ions be estimated selectively. The more stable complex forms first, so the titration can tell them apart.

## Metallurgy: dissolving a metal, then taking it back

Silver and gold are extracted by making them dissolve as complexes. Gold meets cyanide in the presence of oxygen and water and passes into solution as the coordination entity $\ce{[Au(CN)2]-}$. In this ion gold has oxidation state $+1$ and coordination number $2$. The metal is then recovered from the solution by adding zinc, which displaces gold as the free metal.

Purification works in the opposite direction. Impure nickel is turned into the volatile carbonyl $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$, which is then decomposed to leave pure nickel. The carbonyl forms and breaks down depending on conditions, so the nickel travels as a complex and leaves its impurities behind.

## Living systems

Several pigments and enzymes are coordination compounds, with the metal held by large ligands.

| Substance | Role | Metal at the centre |
|---|---|---|
| Chlorophyll | Pigment responsible for photosynthesis | Magnesium |
| Haemoglobin | Red pigment of blood, oxygen carrier | Iron |
| Vitamin $\ce{B12}$ (cyanocobalamine) | Anti-pernicious anaemia factor | Cobalt |
| Carboxypeptidase A | Enzyme, a catalyst of biological systems | Metal ion held in the enzyme |
| Carbonic anhydrase | Enzyme, a catalyst of biological systems | Metal ion held in the enzyme |

Learn the pairings of substance and metal as they stand: magnesium with chlorophyll, iron with haemoglobin, cobalt with vitamin $\ce{B12}$.

## Industry: catalysts and smooth plating

Some coordination compounds are industrial catalysts. The rhodium complex $\ce{[(Ph3P)3RhCl]}$, called Wilkinson catalyst, is used for the hydrogenation of alkenes.

Electroplating gives a second use. Silver and gold deposit much more smoothly and evenly from solutions of the complexes $\ce{[Ag(CN)2]-}$ and $\ce{[Au(CN)2]-}$ than from solutions of the simple metal ions. The complex releases the metal ion slowly, so the coating builds up steadily.

Photography gives a third. In black and white photography, the developed film is fixed by washing with a solution called hypo. Hypo dissolves the silver bromide that light did not decompose, because it forms the complex ion $\ce{[Ag(S2O3)2]^3-}$. Here silver has oxidation state $+1$. Only the unexposed silver bromide is removed, which leaves the image fixed.

## Medicine: chelate therapy

Chelate therapy uses chelating ligands to remove metals that are present in toxic amounts in plant and animal systems. The toxic metal is held in a coordination compound, so it can be carried out of the body.

| Problem | Chelating agent |
|---|---|
| Excess copper and iron | D-penicillamine and desferrioxime B |
| Lead poisoning | EDTA |

Some platinum coordination compounds also inhibit the growth of tumours. The examples NCERT gives are cis-platin and related compounds.

<KeyIdeas>
- Complex formation changes what a metal ion can do, so coordination compounds serve analysis, metallurgy, biology, industry and medicine.
- Colour reactions with chelating ligands such as EDTA, DMG, $\alpha$-nitroso-$\beta$-naphthol and cupron detect and estimate metal ions; $\ce{Ca^2+}$ and $\ce{Mg^2+}$ in hard water are told apart by the stability constants of their EDTA complexes.
- Gold and silver are extracted as complexes such as $\ce{[Au(CN)2]-}$, gold being freed with zinc; impure nickel is purified through $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$, which decomposes to pure nickel.
- Chlorophyll (Mg), haemoglobin (Fe) and vitamin $\ce{B12}$ (Co) are coordination compounds, as are the enzymes carboxypeptidase A and carbonic anhydrase.
- Wilkinson catalyst hydrogenates alkenes, $\ce{[Ag(CN)2]-}$ and $\ce{[Au(CN)2]-}$ give smooth plating, hypo fixes film as $\ce{[Ag(S2O3)2]^3-}$, and chelating agents (D-penicillamine, desferrioxime B, EDTA) and cis-platin serve in medicine.
</KeyIdeas>
