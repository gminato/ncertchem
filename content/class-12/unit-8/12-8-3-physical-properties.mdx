## Gas, volatile liquid or solid?

The simplest aldehyde, methanal, is a gas under ordinary room conditions. Ethanal is a volatile liquid, so it evaporates readily. Heavier members of the family are liquids or solids, and the physical state changes as the molecule grows.

## Boiling points: the pull between molecules

Boiling asks how much energy it takes to lift molecules out of the liquid and into the vapour, and that depends on how strongly neighbouring molecules pull on each other. In a carbonyl compound the C=O bond is polar, with carbon carrying δ⁺ and oxygen carrying δ⁻. Neighbouring molecules therefore attract each other through dipole–dipole forces. The association is weak, but it is enough to lift aldehydes and ketones above hydrocarbons and ethers of comparable molecular mass.

An alcohol of similar mass boils higher still. An O–H group lets alcohol molecules form hydrogen bonds with one another, and a carbonyl compound has no O–H group to do this. That is why its boiling point sits below that of the alcohol.

The five compounds below, all with molecular masses of 58 or 60, put this into order. Their boiling points rise from n-butane (273 K) through methoxyethane (281 K) and propanal (322 K) to acetone (329 K) and propan-1-ol (370 K). The walk-through below climbs this ladder one rung at a time.

## Water, solubility and smell

The lowest members of the family, methanal, ethanal and acetone, mix with water in any proportion. The carbonyl oxygen of each one can take a hydrogen bond from water. Solubility then falls quickly as the alkyl chain lengthens, although every aldehyde and ketone dissolves fairly well in organic solvents such as benzene, ether, methanol and chloroform.

Smell follows a similar pattern. The smallest aldehydes smell sharp and biting. As the molecule grows, the smell turns softer and more fragrant. Several aldehydes and ketones found in nature are used to build perfumes and flavouring blends.

<KeyIdeas>
- Aldehydes and ketones boil higher than hydrocarbons and ethers of similar molecular mass, because their polar C=O groups attract each other through dipole–dipole forces.
- They boil lower than alcohols of similar mass, because they have no O–H group and cannot form hydrogen bonds with one another.
- The simplest aldehyde is a gas, ethanal is a volatile liquid, and most of the others are liquids or solids.
- The lowest members (methanal, ethanal and acetone) mix with water in any proportion, because their carbonyl oxygen can take a hydrogen bond from water.
- Solubility in water falls quickly as the alkyl chain lengthens, and the larger members have softer, more fragrant smells.
</KeyIdeas>
